## 1. Executive summary

Oynk is designed as programmable infrastructure between payment demand and local financial capability. Payment applications use a consistent lifecycle while qualified liquidity and settlement providers execute corridor-specific collection, conversion, digital-asset, and payout legs.

The architecture separates five concerns:

1. **Experience:** public web, payment experiences, and organization-specific operations consoles.
2. **Control plane:** identity, authorization, exact-value validation, compliance policy, quotes, provider selection, payment state, and authoritative references.
3. **Provider plane:** qualified participants that supply liquidity, banking connectivity, collection, exchange, and destination payout.
4. **Settlement plane:** the deployed Soroban MVP and its proposed hardened successor governing funding, commitments, claims, refunds, and disputes.
5. **Visibility plane:** chain indexers, normalized PostgreSQL records, reconciliation, metrics, and operational dashboards.

The design deliberately separates observed blockchain movement from verified business settlement. A chain transaction proves accepted ledger activity; it does not prove customer authorization, provider eligibility, fiat delivery, or end-to-end payment completion.

## 2. Goals and Non-Goals

### Goals

- Provide payment applications with a consistent, idempotent payment lifecycle across multiple liquidity providers and corridors.
- Enable provider discovery, eligibility checks, competitive quotes, rate locking, and liquidity-capacity management.
- Integrate Stellar Anchor Platform for standardized authentication, customer information, cross-border payments, and quotes through SEP-10, SEP-12, SEP-31, and SEP-38.
- Support cross-chain USDC liquidity routing into Stellar through Circle CCTP.
- Enable optional asset conversion into settlement USDC through Soroswap.
- Coordinate payment obligations, escrow, claims, refunds, and disputes through Oynk's Soroban settlement protocol.
- Enforce identity, authorization, compliance, corridor limits, and transaction policies before value-changing operations.
- Maintain exact monetary accounting and consistent references across providers, Anchor transactions, blockchain transfers, and settlement records.
- Provide durable monitoring, reconciliation, audit trails, and recovery from partial failures.
- Keep liquidity providers modular so they can be added, suspended, or replaced without redesigning application integrations.

### Non-Goals

Oynk does not replace regulated banking services, local licensing, sanctions compliance, provider due diligence, or legal obligations.

Oynk does not operate its own decentralized exchange or cross-chain bridge. Soroswap provides asset conversion, while Circle CCTP facilitates supported native USDC cross-chain transfers.

Onchain settlement does not independently prove that an offchain fiat payout occurred. Fiat settlement confirmation requires evidence from authorized providers and must be reconciled separately.

The SCF-funded implementation is limited to testnet development and validation. Live fiat payouts and mainnet commercial settlement are outside the grant-funded milestone scope.

## 3. System Context

### 3.1 SCF Integration Scope at a Glance

Oynk combines provider coordination, liquidity routing, and programmable settlement into a unified payment lifecycle. Each component has a distinct responsibility.

| Building Block | Role in Oynk | Planned Integration Surface | System of Record |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Stellar Anchor Platform** | Standardized authentication, customer information, cross-border payment workflows, and quotes | SEP-10, SEP-12, SEP-31, and SEP-38 through the Oynk business server | Anchor Platform for SEP workflow records; Oynk for provider assignment, payment orchestration, and commercial state |
| **Provider and Corridor Engine** | Provider eligibility, supported corridors, currencies, transaction limits, liquidity capacity, competing quotes, and rate locks | Oynk backend APIs, provider configuration, quote comparison, and reservation lifecycle | Oynk provider registry, quote engine, and payment database |
| **Circle CCTP** | Cross-chain native USDC liquidity routing into Stellar | CCTP messaging, attestations, source transactions, destination minting, and transfer tracking | Supported blockchains for transfer events; Oynk for payment attribution and reconciliation |

| **Soroban Settlement Protocol** | Escrow, settlement obligations, authorization, claims, refunds, disputes, and lifecycle events | Oynk's existing Soroban contracts, hardened with security controls and versioned events | Soroban for onchain settlement state; Oynk for the complete commercial-payment lifecycle |
| **Payment Orchestration and Reconciliation** | Coordinate the complete payment lifecycle and reconcile onchain and offchain records | Stable payment identifiers, provider evidence, Anchor transactions, CCTP transfers, Soroswap conversions, Soroban events, and monitoring | Oynk's payment ledger and reconciliation database, supported by authoritative external records |

### 3.2 Payment Authorization and Wallet Connectivity

Oynk separates wallet access from business authorization.

Participants may use supported Stellar wallets or compatible account-access methods to authorize blockchain transactions. However, wallet ownership alone does not establish eligibility to participate in Oynk's payment network.

Before accepting a payment or settlement instruction, Oynk validates:

- Organization identity and assigned permissions.
- Applicable KYC/KYB and compliance requirements.
- Supported corridors, currencies, and provider eligibility.
- Transaction limits, available liquidity, and rate-lock validity.
- Transaction-specific authorization and settlement conditions.

Oynk's authorization policies remain independent of the wallet connection method.

### 3.3 Integration Boundaries

**Anchor Platform:** Provides standardized Stellar ecosystem interfaces. Oynk's business server implements the application-specific rules, provider coordination, and commercial workflow.

**Circle CCTP:** Transfers native USDC between supported blockchain networks. It does not perform fiat settlement or currency exchange.

**Soroswap:** Executes supported asset conversions on Stellar. It does not manage Oynk's provider obligations or determine commercial payment completion.

**Soroban:** Enforces onchain settlement conditions and records contract events. It does not independently verify offchain fiat payouts.

**Oynk:** Coordinates provider eligibility, competitive quoting, rate locks, payment orchestration, authorized fiat evidence, and reconciliation across these components.

### 3.4 SCF Development Boundary

The grant-funded work covers integration development, Soroban contract hardening, automated testing, security remediation, monitoring, and end-to-end validation on Stellar Testnet.

Independent production deployment and live commercial operations are outside the SCF-funded scope.

## 3.2 End-to-End Integration Flow

Oynk coordinates provider discovery, competitive quoting, liquidity routing, and settlement through a unified payment lifecycle.

```mermaid

flowchart LR
  B["Approved Business"] --> O["Oynk Control Plane"]
  O --> K["Compliance & Corridor Policy"]
  K --> AP["Anchor Platform"]
  AP <--> BS["Oynk Business Server"]
  BS --> Q["Provider & Quote Engine"]
  LP["Liquidity Providers"] --> Q

  Q --> L["Accepted Quote & Rate Lock"]
  L --> F{"Funding Route"}

  F --> U["Direct Stellar USDC"]
  F --> C["Circle CCTP"]
  F --> SW["Soroswap"]

  C --> U
  SW --> U
  U --> S["Soroban Settlement"]

  S --> I["Event Indexer"]
  I --> R["Oynk Reconciliation"]
  FP["Authorized Fiat Provider"] --> R
  AP -.-> R
  R -.-> O
```

The expected payment lifecycle is:

1. **Initiate payment:** An approved business submits a payment request through Oynk.
2. **Validate eligibility:** Oynk checks organization permissions, customer requirements, corridor availability, transaction limits, and provider eligibility.
3. **Discover liquidity:** Eligible providers submit competing quotes based on supported corridors, currencies, available capacity, rates, and fees.
4. **Accept quote:** Oynk validates the selected quote, records the agreed terms, and creates a time-bound rate lock.
5. **Route liquidity:** Settlement funding is prepared through direct Stellar USDC, Circle CCTP, or Soroswap conversion, as applicable.
6. **Execute settlement:** Oynk's Soroban protocol enforces authorized settlement obligations, escrow funding, claims, refunds, and disputes.
7. **Reconcile payment:** Oynk correlates Anchor transactions, provider evidence, liquidity transfers, Soroban events, and internal ledger records.
8. **Confirm completion:** A commercial payment is marked complete only after all required onchain and offchain obligations are satisfied.

## 3.3 Provider, Corridor, Quote and Rate-Lock Engine

Oynk maintains a configurable provider registry supporting multiple payment corridors, currencies, transaction limits, available liquidity, and provider-specific eligibility requirements.

The engine will implement:

- Provider profiles, supported corridors, currencies, transaction limits, and available capacity.
- Eligibility filtering based on compliance, operational status, corridor support, and liquidity availability.
- Competitive quote collection and comparison using exchange rates, fees, and destination amounts.
- Firm quote acceptance with immutable terms and defined expiry.
- Capacity reservation, rate-lock release, and rejection of expired or duplicate acceptances.
- Auditable provider assignment and payment-state transitions.

**Completion test:** At least two simulated providers submit competing quotes for the same eligible payment. Unsupported, suspended, over-limit, and over-capacity providers are excluded. Expired or duplicate quote acceptances cannot create additional obligations.

## 3.4 Stellar Anchor Platform Integration

Oynk integrates Stellar Anchor Platform with its business server to support standardized authentication, customer information, cross-border payment workflows, and quotes.

| SEP | Integration purpose | Oynk responsibility |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **SEP-10** | Stellar account authentication | Verify account identity and bind authorized accounts to approved organizations |
| **SEP-12** | Customer information requirements and status | Coordinate customer information, compliance decisions, and status handling |
| **SEP-31** | Cross-border payment workflows | Validate payment instructions, provider assignments, transaction references, and status changes |
| **SEP-38** | Indicative and firm quotes | Obtain provider pricing, validate terms, enforce quote expiry, and associate accepted quotes with payments |

The Oynk business server will implement authenticated callbacks, schema validation, stable transaction references, idempotent processing, and valid state transitions.

Sensitive customer information and banking records remain offchain.

Anchor transaction creation or status updates do not independently establish fiat payout completion. Oynk requires authenticated provider evidence and reconciliation before confirming commercial finality.

**Completion test:** Demonstrate SEP-10 authentication, SEP-12 customer requirements, SEP-38 firm quotes, and SEP-31 transaction creation and tracking. Verify payment-reference mapping, callback idempotency, invalid-state rejection, and automated integration tests.

**Scope:** SEP-24 is excluded from the current grant-funded integration scope.

## 3.5 Circle CCTP Liquidity Routing

Circle's Cross-Chain Transfer Protocol enables Oynk to route native USDC from supported blockchain networks into Stellar for settlement funding.

The integration will implement:

- Supported source and destination network validation.
- Native USDC transfer initiation and tracking.
- CCTP message identification, attestation tracking, and destination mint verification.
- Stable association with Oynk payment or provider-treasury references.
- Duplicate-processing protection, delayed-transfer handling, and recovery procedures.
- Transfer reconciliation using authoritative blockchain transaction records.

CCTP is a liquidity-routing mechanism. It does not provide foreign-exchange conversion, determine provider eligibility, or confirm fiat settlement.

**Completion test:** Demonstrate a successful test USDC transfer from a supported source network into Stellar, with verifiable source and destination transactions, CCTP message and attestation records, payment attribution, and failure-handling tests.

## 3.6 Soroswap Asset Conversion

Oynk integrates Soroswap as an optional liquidity route for converting supported Stellar assets into settlement USDC.

The integration will implement:

- Supported asset and network validation.
- Swap quote retrieval and route selection.
- Quote expiry, maximum slippage, minimum output, and price-impact controls.
- Transaction execution and output verification.
- Attribution of converted funds to the corresponding payment or provider account.
- Reconciliation of swap transactions, fees, and resulting settlement balances.

Soroswap provides asset conversion. Oynk remains responsible for payment authorization, provider obligations, and commercial settlement status.

**Completion test:** Demonstrate a successful testnet conversion into USDC, verify the resulting transaction and payment attribution, and reject unsupported assets, expired quotes, excessive slippage, and insufficient output.

## 3.7 Soroban Coordination and Settlement Protocol

Oynk already has a functional Soroban settlement MVP. The grant-funded work will harden and extend this implementation rather than recreate it.

The Soroban protocol manages:

- Exact USDC settlement commitments and escrow accounting.
- Authorized participant roles and provider obligations.
- Settlement deadlines, claims, cancellations, refunds, and disputes.
- Contract authorization, replay protection, and valid state transitions.
- Versioned settlement events and auditable contract operations.
- Controlled upgrades, emergency restrictions, and governance permissions.

The control plane keeps customer identity, banking instructions, compliance evidence, provider pricing details, and raw fiat payout evidence offchain.

Contract methods enforce authorized actions, supported assets, exact amounts, deadlines, and permitted transitions. Claims and refunds must not both settle the same obligation.

**Completion test:** Publish the hardened testnet contract, source code, automated tests, reproducible build instructions, WASM hash, and deployment manifest. Demonstrate successful funding and claims, refunds, disputes, authorization failures, duplicate-execution rejection, and event indexing.

Document the improvements relative to the existing MVP.

## 3.8 Cross-Integration Identifiers and State Ownership

Each payment receives a stable `payment_id` used to correlate activity across Oynk, liquidity providers, Anchor Platform, CCTP, Soroswap, and Soroban.

Native identifiers are preserved rather than inferred from transaction amounts or timestamps.

| Reference | Purpose |
| :-- | :-- |
| `payment_id` | Stable Oynk payment identity and idempotency scope |
| `organization_id` | Approved business or provider identity |
| `provider_id` / `provider_assignment_id` | Selected liquidity provider and assigned obligation |
| `corridor_id` | Configured payment corridor and policy |
| `quote_id` / `rate_lock_id` | Selected quote, accepted terms, and expiry |
| `anchor_transaction_id` / `anchor_quote_id` | Anchor Platform transaction and quote references |
| `cctp_transfer_id` / `cctp_message_id` | Cross-chain USDC transfer tracking |
| `swap_transaction_hash` | Soroswap execution evidence |
| `settlement_id` / `soroban_transaction_hash` | Onchain settlement identity and transaction |
| `fiat_reference` | Authorized provider's offchain payment evidence |
| `reconciliation_id` | Reconciliation record and final disposition |

### State ownership

**Oynk:** Commercial payment state, provider assignment, rate locks, policy decisions, and reconciliation.

**Anchor Platform:** Standardized SEP transaction and customer-workflow state.

**Circle CCTP and supported blockchains:** Cross-chain USDC transfer evidence and minting state.

**Soroswap and Stellar:** Executed asset conversions and resulting asset balances.

**Soroban:** Onchain escrow, settlement obligations, and contract-state transitions.

**Authorized fiat providers:** Offchain collection and payout evidence.

A successful operation in one component cannot independently mark the complete commercial payment as settled.

### Reconciliation requirements

Oynk reconciles each required payment leg using exact monetary values, authoritative transaction references, and validated state transitions.

Reconciliation must distinguish completed settlement principal from fees, refunds, duplicate transfers, and internal treasury movements.

Missing, inconsistent, or delayed evidence results in a pending or exception state rather than an unsupported completion claim.

## 3.9 Integration Security and Production Readiness

Oynk applies security and operational controls across the complete payment lifecycle.

### Security requirements

- Organization authorization, role separation, and transaction-specific permissions.
- KYC/KYB, sanctions, corridor, and provider-policy enforcement.
- Exact monetary accounting without floating-point arithmetic.
- Authenticated callbacks, replay protection, and idempotent transaction processing.
- Provider eligibility, liquidity-capacity checks, and rate-lock expiry enforcement.
- CCTP network, recipient, transfer, and destination-mint verification.
- Soroswap asset validation, slippage protection, and minimum-output enforcement.
- Soroban authorization, escrow invariants, upgrade controls, and emergency restrictions.
- Durable event indexing, reconciliation, audit logs, monitoring, and recovery procedures.

### Security review and remediation

Oynk will conduct adversarial testing and contract upgrade validation, apply for an independent external security audit through the SCF Audit Bank, and remediate applicable audit findings.

The external audit is funded separately through the Audit Bank. The Build Award's security allocation covers engineering time for vulnerability remediation, contract fixes, regression testing, and verification.

### Testnet release requirements

The grant-funded release must demonstrate:

1. Reproducible deployment of the integrated testnet infrastructure.
2. Successful end-to-end payment workflows across supported integrations.
3. Automated security, integration, failure-recovery, and reconciliation tests.
4. Monitoring for settlement failures, escrow discrepancies, delayed transfers, and unauthorized changes.
5. Documented security findings, remediation results, operational procedures, and known limitations.
6. Reviewer-accessible contract references, transaction evidence, test results, and technical documentation.

## 6. Experience and presentation layer

The public web application provides Oynk's website and settlement-activity dashboard. The dashboard separates gross blockchain movement from reference-paired estimated settlement, shows inflows and outflows, exposes sync timestamps and timeline activity, and links on-chain observations to explorers.

The authenticated console supports business and settlement-partner signup, email verification, password-plus-OTP sign-in, organization selection, role-specific shells, and business-compliance draft entry. Payment, payout, settlement, provider, liquidity, corridor, terminal, reconciliation, webhook, API-key, and full internal review destinations are not complete transactional products. Business logic must not migrate into React components. UI authorization improves usability but never replaces server enforcement.

## 7. Identity, tenant, and authorization model

### Users and organizations

Users and organizations are distinct records. A user can hold organization memberships; a session selects one active organization and receives the corresponding role and resolved permission list.

Organization types are:

- `BUSINESS`
- `SETTLEMENT_PARTNER`
- `INTERNAL`

Organization lifecycle includes draft, email-verification-required, compliance-incomplete, submitted, under-review, additional-information-required, approved, active, rejected, suspended, and closed states. `SANDBOX`, `TEST`, and `LIVE` platform modes separate environments and operational authority.

### Authentication flow

```mermaid
sequenceDiagram
  participant U as User
  participant C as Console
  participant A as API
  participant DB as PostgreSQL
  participant E as Email provider
  U->>C: Enter email and password
  C->>A: Sign-in request
  A->>DB: Verify normalized user and scrypt hash
  A->>DB: Store hashed, expiring OTP challenge
  A->>E: Deliver OTP
  U->>C: Submit OTP
  C->>A: Verify challenge
  A->>DB: Consume challenge and create hashed session
  A-->>C: HTTP-only session cookie + CSRF token
  C->>A: Select active organization
  A-->>C: User, organization, role and permissions
```

Passwords use scrypt with per-password salts. OTP and session secrets are stored as keyed hashes using an environment pepper. OTPs have expiry, attempt limits, and resend cooldowns. Sessions expire, can be revoked, and carry a distinct CSRF secret. Return paths are constrained to safe local paths. Authentication rate limiting is process-local today and must move to shared gateway or datastore enforcement before horizontal scaling.

### Authorization

Roles map to fine-grained permissions for payments, payouts, terminals, settlements, liquidity, compliance, organization management, members, developer tooling, audit, indexing, applications, and account activation. Every sensitive server route must authenticate the session, resolve the active organization, require the correct permission, and enforce CSRF for cookie-authenticated state changes.

## 8. Compliance architecture

The implemented business profile flow validates legal name, registration information, ISO country codes, incorporation date, tax identifier, website, industry, business description, address, expected volume, expected transactions, and source of funds. A save operation atomically updates the organization, upserts the profile, and records an audit event.

The target control plane adds:

- KYC/KYB and beneficial-owner verification
- Sanctions and politically exposed person screening where required
- Transaction monitoring and case management
- Corridor, currency, asset, provider, and amount/frequency eligibility
- Evidence retention and Travel Rule exchange where applicable
- Versioned decisions linked to payments and assignments

Personal data remains off public chains. On-chain state should contain only stable non-personal references or deliberately designed commitments. The architecture does not imply licensing or approval in any jurisdiction.

## 9. Target payment control plane

The control plane is authoritative for customer intent and business lifecycle. It owns:

- Idempotent payment creation
- Exact origin and destination amounts
- Currency, asset, chain, recipient, and corridor validation
- Quote requests, fees, rate locks, expiry, and slippage
- Compliance and limit decisions
- Provider eligibility, capacity, selection, and assignment
- Funding instructions and deadlines
- Settlement, payout, evidence, exception, refund, and dispute state
- Stable payment, settlement, corridor, and leg references
- Auditable state transitions

Indexers must never create or authorize payment state by observing a similar amount and timestamp.

## 10. Proposed end-to-end payment lifecycle

```mermaid
sequenceDiagram
  participant App as Payment application
  participant O as Oynk control plane
  participant C as Compliance policy
  participant P1 as Origin / liquidity provider
  participant S as Soroban settlement contract
  participant I as Soroban event indexer
  participant P2 as Destination provider
  App->>O: Create request + idempotency key
  O->>C: Evaluate parties, corridor and limits
  C-->>O: Versioned decision
  O->>P1: Request eligible quote and capacity
  P1-->>O: Amount, fees, commitment and expiry
  O-->>App: Quote and expiry
  App->>O: Accept quote idempotently
  O->>P1: Assign origin leg
  P1->>S: Fund exact eligible Stellar asset
  S-->>I: Settlement funded event
  I-->>O: Referenced confirmed state
  O->>P2: Assign destination payout
  P2->>O: Submit payout evidence
```

Completion requires all required legs and evidence. Quote acceptance, funding, provider evidence, on-chain claims, and retries must be idempotent. Expired quotes require requoting. Partial funding requires an explicit top-up, partial acceptance, cancellation, or refund rule. Provider failure can trigger reassignment, refund, or dispute only through defined transitions.

## 11. Provider network architecture

Providers are modular participants, not implicitly trusted extensions of Oynk. Each production profile needs legal/KYB status, beneficial ownership, corridor and currency eligibility, supported assets and rails, banking capability, operating hours, limits, liquidity or collateral policy, pricing, service levels, evidence requirements, contacts, suspension state, and exit procedure.

Assignment filters by eligibility before comparing capacity, price, completion history, concentration, and operational risk. Provider performance should measure verified acceptance, completion time, quote reliability, evidence quality, exception and dispute rates, reconciliation gaps, and availability.

Fiat connectors should expose idempotent create, status, evidence, cancel, and reconcile operations behind provider-specific adapters. A provider callback is untrusted input until authenticated, validated, deduplicated, and correlated to the correct assignment.

## 12. Stellar and Soroban target architecture

Stellar is the proposed asset settlement rail. Soroban is the proposed programmable policy boundary for referenced settlement state. Customer identity, provider due diligence, quote calculation, route selection, compliance reasoning, and detailed fiat evidence remain off-chain.

```mermaid
flowchart LR

  Origin[Origin / liquidity provider] -->|allowed Stellar asset| Contract

  Contract -->|versioned events| EventIndexer[Soroban event indexer]
```

RPC URLs, issuers, network passphrases, contract IDs, explorers, and signers must be environment-specific validated configuration. Testnet and mainnet identifiers must never mix.

## 13. Proposed Soroban settlement contract

### State machine

```mermaid
stateDiagram-v2
  [*] --> Created
  Created --> Funded: exact eligible asset committed
  Created --> Cancelled: cancel before funding
  Funded --> Claimable: authorized payout policy satisfied
  Funded --> Refunding: deadline or cancellation policy
  Funded --> Disputed: authorized dispute
  Claimable --> Claimed: destination claims
  Claimable --> Disputed: challenge within policy window
  Refunding --> Refunded: origin refund succeeds
  Disputed --> Claimed: resolution awards destination
  Disputed --> Refunded: resolution awards origin
  Claimed --> [*]
  Refunded --> [*]
  Cancelled --> [*]
```

### Minimum record

| Field | Function |
| --- | --- |
| `settlement_id` | Unique, stable, non-personal control-plane reference |
| `asset` | Network-scoped asset identity, not a symbol alone |
| `amount` | Exact integer amount under canonical precision rules |
| `origin` and `destination` | Authorized Stellar account or contract addresses |
| `created_at`, `fund_by`, `claim_by` | Ledger-aware time conditions |
| `route_commitment` | Reference/hash for versioned off-chain terms |
| `evidence_commitment` | Optional privacy-preserving evidence commitment |
| `state` | Enumerated lifecycle state |
| authorization policy | Principals allowed to fund, enable, claim, cancel, refund, dispute, resolve, pause, or upgrade |

### Contract invariants

- Settlement identifiers cannot be reused with different terms.
- Only the configured network-scoped asset and permitted amount can fund a request.
- Claim and refund are mutually exclusive terminal outcomes.
- State transitions are monotonic except where an explicitly specified dispute path applies.
- Each transition verifies the necessary Soroban authorization entries.
- Repeated submissions are idempotent or fail without changing value.
- Deadline behavior is based on documented ledger-time semantics.
- Checked integer arithmetic prevents overflow, underflow, and precision drift.
- Stored state and emitted events exclude names, phone numbers, bank data, credentials, and raw compliance evidence.

### Events and indexing

Events should be versioned and cover creation, funding, claim enablement, claim, refund initiation, refund, dispute, resolution, pause, and migration where applicable. The future indexer stores network, contract ID, ledger, transaction hash, event position, topic/version, settlement reference, decoded payload, and ingestion checkpoint. Event identity must be deterministic so replays do not duplicate business records.

## 14. Stellar asset and transaction model

An asset allowlist is keyed by network and canonical identity. Classic Stellar assets require code and issuer. Soroban token use requires the verified contract address and its relationship to the underlying asset. Symbols are display values only.

Before building a transaction, Oynk validates network/passphrase, asset identity, decimals, exact scalar conversion, trustline or authorization prerequisites, issuer controls, recipient address, settlement state, and signer authority.

A dedicated submitter should:

1. Build the intended operation from immutable, versioned instructions.
2. Simulate Soroban execution.
3. Enforce resource and fee limits.
4. Collect the minimum required authorization.
5. Add bounded time conditions and network/contract domain binding.
6. Persist submission intent and transaction identity under an idempotency key.
7. Submit once and recover status after timeouts rather than blindly resubmitting.
8. Wait for the defined successful ledger and contract result.
9. Correlate the indexed event to the authoritative settlement reference.

If Oynk sponsors fees or reserves, sponsorship must be limited by operation, account, asset, amount, frequency, and environment to prevent arbitrary relay abuse.

## 15. Smart accounts and delegated authorization

The planned smart-account layer supports narrow authority rather than unlimited hot keys. A policy may combine a Stellar G-account or contract principal, passkey-backed user authorization, short-lived session delegates, per-action and per-asset limits, recipient constraints, rolling spend limits, expiry, invocation counts, nonces, recovery, and revocation.

Phone numbers can aid discovery or recovery but are not cryptographic authorization.

### Low-connectivity flow

```mermaid
sequenceDiagram
  participant U as User device, temporarily offline
  participant M as Merchant device, online
  participant A as Soroban smart account
  U->>U: Sign bounded, domain-separated authorization
  U-->>M: Transfer signed payload locally
  M->>A: Submit payload and expected nonce
  A->>A: Verify signer, operation, asset, amount, recipient, expiry, nonce and network
  A-->>M: Accept or reject atomically
```

This is one-sided low-connectivity authorization, not fully offline settlement. The merchant must reach the network, and offline users cannot observe recent revocation or competing submissions. Policies require narrow caps, short expiry, atomic nonce consumption, secure key storage, domain separation, clear wallet signing UX, and explicit maximum exposure.

## 16. Current blockchain visibility plane

### Synchronization coordination

```mermaid
flowchart TB
  Trigger[Startup, interval, CLI or admin request] --> Guard[In-process guard]
  Guard --> Lock[PostgreSQL advisory lock]
  Lock --> Run[sync_runs: RUNNING]
  Run --> BSC[BSC wallet × contract scans]
  Run --> SOL[Solana wallet/source scans]
  BSC --> Transfers[(transfers)]
  SOL --> Transfers
  BSC --> State[(sync_state)]
  SOL --> Failures[(indexer_failures)]
  Transfers --> Pairing[Complementary-leg pairing]
  Pairing --> Finish[sync_runs: COMPLETED / PARTIAL / FAILED]
  Finish --> Metrics[Dashboard aggregation]
```

An in-process flag prevents overlapping work locally. A fixed PostgreSQL advisory lock prevents concurrent API or CLI processes from executing the global sync. Every run receives a UUID and durable start/end time, mode, state, counts, error count, and per-chain outcome. Partial success is retained.

### BSC indexing

The BSC indexer observes configured wallets against allowlisted ERC-20 contracts. Current assets are USDT, USDC, and BTCB. It calculates a safe tip by subtracting confirmation depth, rewinds recent blocks on later runs to reconcile reorganizations, and advances a cursor keyed by normalized wallet and contract.

Log ranges adapt to RPC constraints: the indexer begins with a configured chunk, retries temporary failures, reduces rejected ranges down to a minimum, and grows after repeated successes. Delays and jitter reduce provider pressure. Block timestamps are cached within a run. Deterministic transfer identities and upserts make rewind and replay safe.

### Solana indexing

The Solana indexer observes configured owner, associated-token-account, token-account, and manual sources. It fetches signatures, loads parsed transactions, and derives USDT/USDC movement from pre/post token balances. Failed transactions remain visible with `FAILED` status but cannot be paired.

The current signature limit bounds each source scan. Production still needs durable historical pagination/checkpoints, comprehensive source discovery, Token-2022 coverage, and an automatic durable-failure reprocessor. Reliable archival RPCs are required for material backfills.

### Pairing and settlement semantics

The current matcher processes confirmed, unpaired inflows and selects an available confirmed outflow using same-asset preference, amount proximity, and time proximity. PostgreSQL `NUMERIC` performs amount comparison. Both legs are locked and updated within one database transaction.

These pairs are labeled `HEURISTIC` with confidence `0.5000`. They support investigation only and are excluded from authoritative reference-paired settlement. Future payment orchestration must write explicit payment, settlement, corridor, origin-leg, and destination-leg references.

## 17. Exact value and metrics

Raw token integers, decimals, normalized decimal strings, and operational USD values are retained. Monetary calculations must use integer or exact decimal representations.

Current operational valuations are:

| --- | --- | --- |
| BSC | USDT | 1.00 |
| BSC | USDC | 1.00 |
| BSC | BTCB | 25,000.00 fixed estimate |
| Solana | USDT | 1.00 |

BTCB's value is not live or historical pricing. Metrics are operational indicators, not accounting statements.

- **Gross transfer volume:** all confirmed observed legs plus recorded off-chain movement.
- **Inflow and outflow:** wallet-relative direction.
- **Estimated settled volume:** conservative smaller value of `REFERENCE`-paired legs plus off-chain records.
- **Unmatched volume:** movement not included in authoritative pairing.
- **Settlement count:** reference-paired settlements plus off-chain records.
- **Last indexed time:** synchronization-run time, not transaction time.

## 20. Trust boundaries and security model

1. Credentials prove identity; organization membership and permission checks grant authority.
2. Browser checks never replace API authorization.
3. Provider evidence is authenticated external input, not self-proving completion.
4. Chain consensus proves ledger state, not off-chain identity or payout truth.
5. RPC responses and webhook payloads are untrusted and validated before persistence.
6. Public reporting minimizes personal and commercially sensitive data.
7. Signing material, API keys, session tokens, OTPs, signed payloads, and private evidence are never logged.
8. Mainnet and testnet configuration, identifiers, signers, and state remain strictly partitioned.

Current controls include Helmet security headers, configured CORS, JSON body limits, Zod input validation, scrypt password hashing, HMAC-secret storage, timing-safe comparisons, CSRF checks, role permissions, audit records, admin sync authentication, replay-safe indexing identities, confirmation depth, rewind, advisory locking, and durable failure state.

Before production, add shared rate limiting, stronger operator endpoint authorization, key rotation, secret management, RPC failover, anomaly alerts, complete audit coverage, controlled CORS, formal data retention, and independent security review.

## 21. Soroban threat model

| Threat | Architectural control |
| --- | --- |
| Unauthorized claim/refund | Explicit auth graph, least privilege, mutually exclusive terminal states, invariant tests |
| Cross-action/network replay | Domain-separated payload, nonce, schema, contract, and network binding |
| Stale quote/deadline use | Ledger-time checks, bounded expiry, eligibility revalidation |
| Asset substitution | Network-scoped issuer/contract allowlist; never symbol-only identity |
| Precision or arithmetic defect | Canonical scalar rules, checked integer arithmetic, boundary/property tests |
| Reentrancy/callback surprise | Minimal external calls and adversarial contract tests |
| Upgrade-key compromise | Multisignature governance, limited authority, timelock where appropriate, rollback policy |
| Privacy leakage | References/commitments only; no personal or bank data in storage/events |
| Event/indexer divergence | Deterministic event identity, ledger checkpoints, replay, independent reconciliation |
| RPC outage/censorship | Multiple providers, durable submissions, backoff, status recovery, alerts |
| Offline authorization double use | Atomic nonce consumption, narrow caps, short expiry, merchant confirmation |

## 22. Reliability, scaling, and observability

- Adaptive range sizing and bounded RPC retry
- PostgreSQL advisory locks
- Durable run and failure records

### Scale path

Move indexing out of the API into workers partitioned by chain and source under durable leases. Preserve deterministic uniqueness and transactional checkpoint rules. Alerts should cover stalled cursors, aging failure backlog, repeated reorg replay, RPC latency/rate limits, partial runs, database pool saturation, stale readiness, unexpected auth failures, Soroban event lag, unresolved contract states, and control-plane/chain divergence.

## 24. Disaster recovery and reconciliation

PostgreSQL requires encrypted backups, point-in-time recovery, defined RPO/RTO, retained migration history, and recurring restoration tests. Preserve control-plane, provider, compliance, submission, event, evidence, and audit records according to policy.

Derived blockchain data can be rebuilt from verified checkpoints and bounded backfills, but RPC archival availability cannot be the sole backup. After restoration, reconcile database state against BSC safe tips, Solana signatures, future Stellar ledgers, Soroban contract state/events, provider evidence, and control-plane terminal states before reopening value-changing operations.

## 29. Architectural conclusion

Oynk's core architectural principle is separation of authority. Applications express intent; the control plane owns policy and lifecycle; providers execute bounded legs; Soroban is intended to enforce narrowly defined on-chain settlement transitions; indexers report ledger facts; and reconciliation determines whether all referenced obligations agree.

The current repository provides meaningful identity, compliance, observability, indexing, and presentation foundations, and the separate mainnet MVP proves execution of the initial Soroban settlement lifecycle. Oynk is not yet an end-to-end, audited Stellar payment platform. Moving production funds safely requires integrating and hardening the contract and control-plane layers, preserving exactness and replay safety, proving authorization invariants, independently reviewing the system, and launching within narrow operational and regulatory limits.
